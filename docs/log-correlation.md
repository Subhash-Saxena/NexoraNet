## 1. Multi-Event Correlation Principles

Isolated log events often look benign in isolation (e.g. a single failed password attempt). An enterprise SIEM correlates multiple related events across time and systems to discover malicious patterns.

The NexoraNet Correlation Engine implements:
* **Sliding Time Windows**: Events must occur within `N` seconds of each other.
* **Aggregation Keys**: Grouping by `username`, `source_ip`, `destination_ip`, or `host`.
* **Threshold Counts**: Triggering only when matching occurrences reach or exceed a critical threshold.
* **State & Sequence Detection**: Validating conditions like multiple failures followed by a single success.

## 3. Correlation Alert Lifecycle

```text
       [RULE MATCH]
             │
             ▼
        STATUS: NEW
             │
   ┌─────────┼─────────┐
   ▼         ▼         ▼
ACKNOWLEDGED ESCALATED FALSE_POSITIVE
   │         │
   ▼         ▼
RESOLVED  SOC / HUNT
```

1. **NEW**: Alert generated by engine; unreviewed by analyst.
2. **ACKNOWLEDGED**: Analyst has claimed the alert and initiated triage.
3. **ESCALATED**: Transferred to a Step 12 SOC Investigation (`INV-YYYY-XXXX`) or Step 14 Threat Hunt (`HUNT-YYYY-XXXX`).
4. **RESOLVED**: Remediated, verified, or closed.
5. **FALSE_POSITIVE**: Analyst documented benign operational cause (e.g. expired automation credentials).

## 4. Dry-Run Rule Testing

Students can execute a rule in "Dry-Run" mode:
* Evaluates rule logic against any selected dataset.
* Reports total matching occurrences and sample events.
* Never produces real alerts in the production queue, enabling safe experimentation.
